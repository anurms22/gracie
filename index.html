Who Is Gracie Abrams?

The full story — from Pacific Palisades bedroom to arena stages worldwide

The Basics

Gracie Madigan Abrams was born on September 7, 1999, in Los Angeles, California, and raised in Pacific Palisades — one of LA's most storied neighborhoods, and one that would be devastated by the January 2025 wildfires she'd later perform a benefit show for. She is the daughter of filmmaker J.J. Abrams (Lost, Star Wars: The Force Awakens, Mission: Impossible III) and film and television producer Katie McGrath, and the granddaughter of television producers Gerald W. and Carol Ann Abrams. Her paternal heritage is Ashkenazi Jewish; her maternal background is Irish Catholic. She has two brothers: an older brother Henry and a younger brother August.

Early Life & Education

Gracie developed an interest in music from a very young age — she wrote her first song at age eight, inspired by her mother playing Joni Mitchell in the house. She attended The Archer School for Girls in West Los Angeles, graduating in 2018. She then enrolled at Barnard College in New York City to study international relations, but left after her first year to pursue music full-time. She's never looked back. Growing up, she posted covers and original songs to Instagram and SoundCloud, slowly building an audience who found her through the specificity and emotional rawness of even her earliest recordings. One of those early fans was Lorde, who DM'd her asking for the MP3 of "minor" after discovering her online.

The Artist

Gracie's music lives in a particular emotional territory: the messy, unglamorous, hyper-specific interior of being young, in love, out of love, and trying to understand yourself. Her signature is a whispered vocal delivery that feels less like a performance and more like she's thinking aloud — confessional to the cellular level. Critics have drawn comparisons to Taylor Swift's storytelling and Phoebe Bridgers' emotional openness, but Gracie's voice and perspective are distinctly her own. She has cited Taylor Swift, Joni Mitchell, Phoebe Bridgers, Elliott Smith, Simon & Garfunkel, Bon Iver, Kate Bush, Lana Del Rey, Robyn, the 1975, Lorde, and Metric as key influences. Her music is most often classified as bedroom pop, folk-pop, indie pop, and indie folk — but it transcends any single label because the emotional specificity is always the point, not the genre.

The Career Arc
2019

Signs to Interscope — releases "Mean It"

Her debut single arrives at age 19. Lorde has already championed her. The industry notices.

2020

Releases minor EP — inspires "drivers license"

Her debut EP drops in July. Olivia Rodrigo hears "I miss you, I'm sorry" and writes "drivers license." The music world doesn't fully realize it yet, but two careers are about to intertwine.

2021

This Is What It Feels Like EP — meets Aaron Dessner

Her second EP arrives in November, with "Rockland" marking her first full collaboration with The National's Aaron Dessner. A creative partnership that will define her career is born.

2022

Opens for Olivia Rodrigo's SOUR Tour — a full-circle moment

The girl who inspired "drivers license" opens for its creator. Simultaneously running her own This Is What It Feels Like Tour. Breaks up with Blake Slatkin. Starts making Good Riddance.

2023

Good Riddance debuts — Grammy nomination — opens Eras Tour

Her debut album arrives February 24. Her Good Riddance Tour sells out in under one hour. She opens for Taylor Swift on select Eras Tour dates, performing for stadium crowds of 60,000+. Grammy nomination for Best New Artist. Named to Forbes 30 Under 30.

2024

The Secret of Us — #1 in five countries — "That's So True" breaks through

The Secret of Us drops June 21, featuring a collaboration with Taylor Swift on "us." It debuts at #2 on the Billboard 200 and #1 in the UK, Australia, Canada, the Netherlands, and Scotland. "That's So True" tops charts in five countries and peaks at #6 on the US Hot 100. She opens the final Eras Tour North American legs and makes her SNL debut in December. Grammy nominated for Best Pop Duo for "us."

2025

Arena headliner — MSG debut — Lollapalooza headliner — wins AMA New Artist

Her Secret of Us Tour goes global across 4 continents, 83 shows. She makes her Madison Square Garden debut. Headlines Lollapalooza with Robyn as a surprise guest. Performs at Glastonbury with Paul Mescal in the crowd. Wins American Music Award for New Artist of the Year (May 26). AMA wins, a Chanel global campaign, and a debut A24 film role announced. Begins teasing a third album.

2026

Daughter from Hell era begins — "Hit The Wall" out May 14

Third album announced with lead single "Hit The Wall." Confirmed collaboration with Bon Iver. Film debut in A24's Please (directed by Halina Reijn). Named global face of Chanel Coco Crush jewelry. Attends Oscars with Paul Mescal in matching black.

Her Sound & Influences

Joni Mitchell

Her most cited formative influence — she has a Joni tattoo and credits her mother for playing Joni around the house. The confessional, hyper-specific lyrical style owes a direct debt.

Taylor Swift

The "Taylor Swift school of songwriting" is a real reference point — detailed narrative, confessional vulnerability, and making personal experience universally felt. Aaron Dessner is the connective tissue.

Phoebe Bridgers

She discovered Phoebe at age 13 and it changed how she heard what songwriting could be. The whispered indie-folk emotional register owes a clear debt.

Elliott Smith

The acoustic intimacy, the self-awareness bordering on self-destruction, the way beauty and pain coexist in the same line — she's cited Elliott as formative.

Bon Iver

Justin Vernon's atmospheric production influence shows up in Aaron Dessner's work — and now Vernon himself is on Gracie's third album.

Robyn

An unlikely influence that explains Gracie's dance floor heart under all the indie-folk — she's covered "Dancing on My Own" multiple times live, most memorably with Robyn herself at Lollapalooza 2025.

1
Feels Like ★ Lead Single
The EP's opener and lead single — an immediate escalation in sonic scope. Feels Like captures the terrifying early stages of falling for someone, the electricity of it mixed with the dread of what happens if it goes wrong. Bright, shimmering production marks Gracie's first serious collaboration with Aaron Dessner.
2
Rockland ★ Single · co-produced by Aaron Dessner
Named after Rockland County, New York — where Aaron Dessner's Long Pond Studio is located. A wistful, place-specific song about wanting to return somewhere that felt like home but no longer quite is. Gracie's first deep collaboration with Dessner, who'd go on to define her next three projects.
3
For Real This Time
A confrontation with yourself: the specific kind of self-deception where you tell yourself you're over something while clearly not being over it at all. The "for real this time" of the title has a weary, ironic edge — she's said this before and doesn't quite believe herself.
4
Camden
A song built around a specific place, filled with quiet observations that accumulate into something heartbreaking. Gracie excels at this kind of hyper-specific geography of feeling — naming a place as a way of naming an emotion that doesn't have a word.
5
The Bottom
One of the EP's darker emotional centers — an honest look at rock-bottom moments and the strange intimacy they create. "I told you I was down bad" is an early example of Gracie's willingness to render herself unglamorously, which is part of what makes her fanbase trust her so completely.
6
Wishful Thinking
A gentle but devastating song about staying in something past its expiration date — not because you believe it'll work, but because wanting it to work is its own comfort. Soft, patient production that matches the feeling of dragging something out.
7
Older
A meditation on aging and the loss of a particular kind of innocence — the version of yourself that believed everything would be fine. Gracie's gift for compression is in full force here: this is a song about years of slow change distilled into a few clean, aching lines.
8
Better
The measured, adult voice on the EP — a song about recognizing a pattern in yourself and wanting, genuinely, to break it. Quieter and more internal than the surrounding tracks, with production that matches its contemplative mood.
9
Hard To Sleep
Exactly what it sounds like — insomnia as emotional symptom, the racing mind at 3am that replays every wrong turn. One of the EP's most relatable tracks and a fan favorite on early setlists.
10
Augusta
Another place-as-feeling song. Augusta carries the specific melancholy of wanting to leave somewhere before you actually leave — the pre-emptive homesickness of a person on the edge of a major change. Many fans interpret it as being about leaving for college or leaving a relationship.
11
Painkillers
A song about numbing yourself — but not in a simple or moralistic way. More interested in the texture of why someone reaches for numbness and what that actually feels like from inside the experience. One of the EP's most sonically interesting cuts.
12
Alright
The EP's quiet resolution — the specific flavor of "alright" that means "I'm surviving, not thriving, and I'm choosing to accept that for now." A gentle, honest close that doesn't promise too much. One of the tracks Gracie frequently mentions as personally meaningful.
Debut Album
Good Riddance 2023
Album · 12 Tracks · Grammy Nominated
Good Riddance
Released: February 24, 2023 · Produced by Aaron Dessner (all tracks) · Interscope Records
Grammy nomination: Best New Artist (66th Grammys, 2024)
2023
1
Best
The album's opening statement and an immediate gut-punch: "You were there all the time / You're the worst of my crimes / You fell hard / I thought good riddance." A song about the strange relief that comes after a relationship finally ends — not joy, exactly, but the particular exhale of someone who's been holding their breath for too long. Sets the album's themes of accountability and self-reckoning in motion instantly.
2
I know it won't work ★ Single
A fan-beloved track about knowing full well that something isn't going to work but being unable to stop yourself from trying anyway. The title is the whole thesis, and the song earns it — building a case for the specific irrationality of hope. Reached the Pop Airplay chart despite not cracking the Hot 100.
3
Full machine
A more expansive, guitar-driven track — one of the album's few moments where the production swells rather than whispers. Explores the exhaustion of performing normalcy when you're anything but: the "full machine" of the title is a person running on empty, keeping everything moving through sheer mechanical momentum.
4
Where do we go now? ★ Single · MV
Released as the album's second single alongside an accompanying music video. A post-breakup question addressed directly to an ex — not accusatory, but genuinely uncertain. One of the album's more outward-facing tracks, less about Gracie's interior and more about the shared wreckage two people leave behind. Billboard named it one of the 96 best songs of 2023.
5
I should hate you
The album's clearest portrait of post-breakup cognitive dissonance — the simultaneous desire to hate someone and the frustrating inability to do so. Gracie approaches it without self-pity, which makes it land harder: she's not asking for sympathy, just naming something true.
6
Will you cry?
A song that dares to ask a question that's usually considered undignified to ask: will you actually mourn this when it's gone? The vulnerability of needing to know you mattered. One of the album's quieter tracks but one that hits disproportionately hard for how understated it is.
7
Amelie ★ Single
Named after a person, not the film — a song about a woman Gracie met once who "sort of ripped her open." Fans have long speculated who Amelie is; Gracie has been characteristically cryptic. Variety named it one of the 35 best songs of 2023. Notably, this song was debuted live at a soundcheck in Copenhagen in May 2022 — months before Good Riddance was announced.
8
Difficult ★ Lead Single · MV
Released in October 2022 as the first official signal that a debut album was coming. A song about being difficult to love — but not in the self-deprecating way that usually framing implies. More like: being complex, requiring work, and wondering if you've asked too much. One of her most-streamed tracks and a live fan favorite.
9
This is what the drugs are for
Gracie's most emotionally raw track on the album — and one of the darkest. Not a romanticization of substance use, but a clinical, unflinching acknowledgment of the specific emotional pain that makes someone reach for numbing. Written with Aaron Dessner and co-produced with Brian Eno's influence on the adjacent track.
10
Fault line
The geological metaphor is perfect: a fault line is stable right up until it isn't, and the disaster that follows has been building for ages. Gracie applies this to the slow unraveling of a relationship — the invisible accumulation of damage beneath the surface.
11
The blue
Named after someone specific — "You live in Hollywood, you're half an hour from me" — but the proximity feels like distance. One of the album's most atmospheric tracks. The color blue here is less about sadness and more about the particular tonal quality of feeling disconnected from someone physically close.
12
Right now (co-written/produced with Brian Eno)
The album's closing track and its most unusual collaboration — co-written and co-produced with ambient music legend Brian Eno. A reflective, almost meditative close that asks what remains when all the noise settles. Gracie working with Eno is one of the album's most surprising and rewarding creative leaps.

The Secret of Us 2024
Album · 13 Tracks · Grammy Nominated
The Secret of Us
Released: June 21, 2024 · Produced by Gracie Abrams & Aaron Dessner · Interscope Records
#2 Billboard 200 · #1 UK, Australia, Canada, Netherlands, Scotland
Grammy nom: Best Pop Duo/Group Performance ("us" feat. Taylor Swift, 67th Grammys)
2024
1
Felt Good About You
The album's opening track — a bittersweet reflection on a relationship that felt right while it was happening, even if it couldn't last. Gracie opens her sophomore record not with anger or grief, but with a kind of warm, complicated nostalgia. It's the emotional equivalent of picking up an old photo and smiling even though it hurts.
2
Risk ★ Single · MV
The album's lead single — a slick, confident pop track about the terrifying calculus of deciding whether someone is worth the emotional risk. Co-written with Audrey Hobert; the music video (also directed by Hobert) features Gracie getting wine-drunk and stalking a crush with increasing determination and decreasing dignity. A crowd favorite on tour.
3
Blowing Smoke
A song about someone who talks a big game but consistently fails to follow through. "I cut the rope and you fell from the tower" — Gracie at her most controlled and pointed. The production here is one of the album's most textured moments: layers of sound that accumulate like the empty promises the song is documenting.
4
I Love You, I'm Sorry ★ Single · MV
Both a sequel to "I miss you, I'm sorry" (2020) and a completely different emotional register: where the original was raw grief, this is wry, self-aware accountability. "I like to slam doors closed / Trust me, I know it's always about me." The "Asshole of the Year" award in the music video is one of her most iconic visual moments. Rolling Stone's 60th best song of 2024. Career-best bridge, per critics.
5
us. feat. Taylor Swift ★ Grammy Nominated
The album's most anticipated track and one of the most discussed pop collaborations of 2024. Taylor Swift contributes vocals to this intimate, confessional track about a relationship built on shared secrets — the "secret of us." Performed live for the first time at Wembley Stadium on June 23, 2024 (night three of Eras Tour), with Gracie on piano and Taylor on guitar. Nominated for Best Pop Duo/Group Performance at the 67th Grammys.
6
Let It Happen
A mid-album turning point — a song about choosing not to fight a feeling for once, about letting something unfold without trying to control or anticipate it. Given how much of Gracie's catalog is about analyzing emotions to their cellular level, "let it happen" reads as an intentional, hard-won choice.
7
Tough Love
"I took a train to Boston and I wanted to" — Gracie at her most direct. A song about the particular cruelty of being loved by someone who expresses it through criticism, withdrawal, or impossible standards. The tenderness in her delivery makes the subject matter hit harder by contrast.
8
I Knew It, I Know You
A song built on the disorienting feeling of being proven right about something you hoped you were wrong about. The title's tense-shift — from past to present — mirrors the emotional experience: the sick satisfaction of "I knew it" followed immediately by the grief of "and now I really know."
9
Gave You I Gave You I
One of the album's most unusual title constructions — and sonically, one of its most interesting. A song about the asymmetry of devotion: giving everything and receiving something significantly less in return. The repetitive title mirrors the exhaustion of the giving.
10
Normal Thing
A song about desperately wanting what you feel to be ordinary — to have it confirmed that this kind of intensity, this kind of pain, is something everyone goes through. The comfort of being told you're not uniquely broken. One of the album's quietest and most emotionally precise tracks.
11
Good Luck Charlie
Named not after the Disney Channel show but after a specific person — a sardonic farewell to someone who treated her badly. The "good luck" is not genuine. "Charlie" is believed by fans to be a deliberate pseudonym. One of the album's more defiant moments and a live crowd-pleaser.
12
Free Now
The final standard-edition track before the closer — a song about the strange, bittersweet feeling of freedom after a relationship ends. Not triumphant freedom. The kind that feels like standing in an empty room and not knowing what to do with all the space.
13
Close to You ★ Single
The album's official closer and one of its most beloved tracks — originally recorded as a 20-second voice memo snippet seven years before the album's release. Fan response to that snippet was so overwhelming that Gracie revisited and finished it specifically for The Secret of Us. About the specific gravitational pull of someone you can't get out of your orbit.

Cool ★ Deluxe
The deluxe's most pointed track — a bold, confessional tell-all about a relationship that soured, delivered with increasing intensity that critics described as one of Gracie's most direct performances. The song that changed everything. A deeply personal song about the vulnerability of confiding in someone and then watching them become someone who no longer deserves what you shared with them. The intimacy of "I told you things" as a grievance — the betrayal of shared secrets. A fan and critic favorite from the deluxe.
17
Packing It Up ★ Deluxe
The deluxe edition's emotional resolution — a song about the specific, physical act of leaving: packing up your things, removing your presence from someone's space, making tangible what was previously only emotional. A quiet, bittersweet close to the extended album.

The creative family, industry orbit, and friendships that shaped Gracie's world

Her Ride or Die — Audrey Hobert
AH

Audrey Hobert — best friend, co-writer, music video director

Gracie's closest creative partner and best friend — and arguably the most important person in the Secret of Us era. Hobert (whose brother is musician Malcolm Todd) and Gracie have been friends since middle school, where they spent their afternoons making Video Star movies together. Hobert made her official songwriting debut co-writing seven tracks on The Secret of Us, including "Risk," "I Love You, I'm Sorry," and "That's So True" — making her one of the most consequential new co-writers in pop. She also directed the music videos for "Risk" and "I Love You, I'm Sorry," bringing a genuinely intimate, playful visual language to Gracie's work. When Gracie talks about the people she trusts most in the world, Hobert is always near the top of the list. Their friendship gives The Secret of Us its particular warmth — you can feel a real human relationship behind the songs.

Fan Theories & Song Meanings

Who the songs are about, what the lyrics really mean, and the detective work fans have done

The Blake Slatkin Era (minor & Good Riddance)

"I miss you, I'm sorry," "Long Sleeves," "21" — The Blake Slatkin Songs

Blake Slatkin, Gracie's boyfriend from approximately 2017–2022 and the producer of her debut EP, is widely believed to be the subject of the minor EP's emotional core. The timeline aligns: Gracie wrote minor "in response to a very transitional year" and "her first breakup" — which would be her relationship with Slatkin. The songs are careful not to name him, but the specificity of "I miss fighting in your old apartment / Breaking dishes when you're disappointed" (I miss you, I'm sorry) feels acutely autobiographical. Gracie has never confirmed or denied, which is part of her artistic approach — the songs are hers, not reportage.

"Best," "I know it won't work," "The blue," "Fault line" — Good Riddance as breakup document

Good Riddance was explicitly described by Gracie as an album written about a breakup, friendships, and family — and the breakup in question is almost certainly her 2022 split from Slatkin. Songs like "Best" ("You're the worst of my crimes / You fell hard / I thought good riddance") and "I should hate you" trace the emotional arc of ending a long relationship that was also a creative partnership. The unusual intimacy of "the blue" ("You live in Hollywood, you're half an hour from me") suggests a specific geography — LA, where both Gracie and Slatkin are based. "Will you cry?" asks whether an ex will mourn you — a very human, vulnerable question that fans read as addressed to Slatkin specifically.

"Amelie" — The Mystery Woman

One of the most intriguing mysteries in Gracie's catalog: "I met a girl once / She sort of ripped me open." Fans have long tried to identify who "Amelie" is. Gracie has been characteristically silent about this one — it's clearly a specific person who made a significant emotional impact, but whether it's a romantic connection, a friendship that ended painfully, or something else entirely remains unknown. The name "Amelie" may be a pseudonym. This is one of the songs fans most want answered.

"That's So True" — The Dylan O'Brien Theory

The most heavily theorized song in Gracie's catalog. "That's So True" describes watching an ex move on to someone new, with lyrics about "looking into big blue eyes" — which fits descriptions of Sabrina Carpenter. Dylan O'Brien (blue eyes, Teen Wolf actor) was reportedly seen with Gracie in April 2022; months later, he was linked to Sabrina Carpenter; subsequently, O'Brien had a new girlfriend (Rachel Lange). Fan theory: Gracie wrote "That's So True" about watching O'Brien's romantic orbit post-their rumored connection, with the "big blue eyes" line as a direct reference to his appearance or to the women he moved on to. Gracie has neither confirmed nor denied. Neither has O'Brien. Notably, Sabrina Carpenter is believed to have written "Opposite" about O'Brien around the same time — creating an unlikely fan-analyzed love triangle.

"Cool" — The Name-Drop Song

The Secret of Us Deluxe track that fans dissect most forensically. Lyrics appear to reference specific women by association — fan analysis suggests references to Sadie Sink, Elle Fanning, and others connected to Dylan O'Brien's dating history. The song is a "tell-all" about a partner who treated Gracie badly, delivered with increasing vocal intensity. If the Dylan O'Brien reading is correct, "Cool" is the more explicitly pointed companion to "That's So True" — less about the emotional experience of watching someone move on and more about documenting why the relationship ended.

"us." feat. Taylor Swift — The Friendship Song

Notably, "us." is one of Gracie's most collaborative songs lyrically — and it reads differently from her solo work. The "secret of us" is widely interpreted by fans as the intimacy of a specific relationship, but which one? Some fans read it as about Taylor and Gracie's own friendship — the inside-knowledge intimacy of their creative relationship. Others read it as a romantic relationship that both artists are speaking from. Gracie has said the collaboration emerged naturally from sessions where Taylor was present, making it a genuine co-creation rather than a feature request.

"I Love You, I'm Sorry" — Accountability to Blake?

As a sequel to "I miss you, I'm sorry" — the signature Blake-era song — "I Love You, I'm Sorry" represents a different emotional register. Where the original was about longing and grief, this one is about self-aware accountability: "I like to slam doors closed / Trust me, I know it's always about me." Many fans read this as Gracie's mature reckoning with her own role in the relationship's end — the same breakup seen from a different angle and with a few more years of reflection. The line "You were the best but you were the worst / As sick as it sounds, I loved you first" reads as deeply personal.

"Out of Nowhere" (unreleased) — About Paul Mescal?

Gracie debuted this unreleased song at Glastonbury 2025 — where Paul Mescal was visibly in the crowd dancing on her shoulders. Fans immediately theorized that "Out of Nowhere" is about the unexpected way Mescal entered her life — the title fitting the narrative of meeting someone through an unlikely circumstance (both being in London in summer 2024, crossing paths in the cultural orbit of Taylor Swift's Eras Tour). This is the most speculated-about unreleased song heading into the third album era.

"Close to You" — The Seven-Year Song

This one has a documented origin: Gracie originally recorded a 20-second snippet of "Close to You" seven years before the album's release. Fan response to that snippet was so intense and sustained over the years that she finished the song specifically because of them. Who was it originally about? The timeline suggests it was written around 2017 — possibly about the early stages of her relationship with Blake Slatkin, or possibly about someone else entirely from her late teens. Gracie revisiting it years later gave the song an additional layer of meaning: a song about gravitational pull returning to its singer long after she thought it was done.

"Good Luck Charlie" — Who is Charlie?

Gracie has been explicit that "Charlie" is a real person — and that "good luck" is not sincere. Fans have speculated extensively but no consensus candidate has emerged. The name appears to be a pseudonym (Gracie is careful about real names, as with "Amelie"). The song's sardonic energy is slightly different from the rest of The Secret of Us — less reflective, more cleanly dismissive. Fan theories include both romantic interests and industry figures who treated her dismissively. The Disney Channel show connection has been explicitly ruled out.

Dating History

Gracie's romantic timeline — and how it maps onto her music

Gracie's most significant documented relationship — they were together for approximately five years, from around 2017 through 2022. Slatkin is a successful songwriter and producer who has worked with The Kid LAROI, Lil Nas X, and Justin Bieber. Songs believed to be about Slatkin: "I miss you, I'm sorry," "21," "Long Sleeves," "Best," "I know it won't work," "Will you cry?," "The blue," "Fault line," and "I Love You, I'm Sorry."

Dylan O'Brien — Actor

The most fan-theorized relationship in Gracie's history — and the one that has most visibly influenced her music.
